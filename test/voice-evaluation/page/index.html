Voice evaluation

Speed is not quality. The benchmarks say how fast a voice runs and then throw the audio away — this page keeps it, beside the text it was made from, so the judgement can be made by a person with headphones on.

    Real-time factor by summary length

    Speaking pace by summary length

    Pace decides how many bytes a day costs, because a slower voice says the same words over more seconds. The marked line is the aggregate this corpus measured. A pace that drifts with length would mean the figure cannot be carried across the corpus as one number.

    Every clip

    The reading behind the charts. Chunks are the count the pipeline split a summary into; each one pays a fixed cost, which is the likeliest reason the shortest sample is the slowest in real-time terms.

    Four scores, four open questions

    These are not generic audio-quality axes. Each one closes a gap the CI-runner benchmark left open in writing, so a filled-in evaluation answers a question somebody already asked rather than producing a number nobody wanted. Three is the pass mark: a three ships, a two does not.

    ScoreThe question it closes
    Intelligibility Could every word be made out without replaying? The benchmark says nothing yet about whether the audio is good enough to publish at all.
    Pronunciation Names, numbers, acronyms, tickers. The corpus is news summaries, so proper nouns are the load-bearing case — a voice that reads them wrong is unusable however pleasant it sounds.
    Prosody Sentence rhythm, emphasis, pauses. Does it read like a person reading news, or like a list of words?
    Seams Are the chunk joins audible? The pipeline splits every summary over forty words, so a seam is an artefact this project creates and therefore one it owns. Five means no join could be heard.

    Defects carry a timestamp

    A note saying “sounds a bit off” cannot be acted on. A defect pinned to at 12.4s with what was heard and what was expected can be found again, fixed, and checked. The Mark button stamps the dock’s current position, so the way to log one is to listen until it happens and press it.

    Where the scores go

    Nothing leaves this page on its own. Scores persist to this browser’s local storage as you work, and Download evaluation writes a JSON file shaped by evaluation-schema.json. That file is the artefact — it stands alone once the clips are gone, because it copies the subject it judged.

    What this page is not

    It does not drive CI and it is not the published Listen surface. It reuses that surface’s component vocabulary — one docked player rather than one per row, the ProgressTrack groove, the inherited tokens — so what is learned here transfers instead of being thrown away.
